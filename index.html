Rome: The Origins

Legend says that Ascanius, son of the Trojan hero Aeneas, founded Alba Longa. His descendants ruled until Amulius overthrew his brother Numitor and forced his daughter Rhea Silvia to become a Vestal Virgin.

The god Mars made her the mother of twins: Romulus and Remus. The babies were abandoned on the Tiber, found by a she-wolf on Palatine Hill, and raised by a shepherd.

Later, Romulus killed Remus after a dispute, and founded Rome in 753 BC.

📍 Palatine Hill 📍 Albano Laziale (Alba Longa)
  • Romulus (753–716 BC): Founder and first king of Rome.
  • Numa Pompilius (715–673 BC): Sabine origin, established many religious rites, reformed the calendar.
  • Tullus Hostilius (673–641 BC): Warlike king, conquered Alba Longa.
  • Ancus Marcius (640–616 BC): Founded Ostia, built the Pons Sublicius.
  • Tarquinius Priscus (616–578 BC): Etruscan origin, promoted public works and military campaigns.
  • Servius Tullius (578–534 BC): Created the Centuriate Assembly, reorganized territorial tribes.
  • Tarquinius Superbus (534–509 BC): Tyrant, overthrown, Republic founded.
