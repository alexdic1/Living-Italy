The Founding of Rome

Legend says that Ascanius, son of the Trojan hero Aeneas (descendant of Venus and a mortal), founded Alba Longa, an ancient city near the River Tiber. His descendants ruled there for generations, until two brothers, Numitor and Amulius, fought for the throne. Amulius took power and forced Numitor's daughter, Rhea Silvia, to become a Vestal Virgin, sworn to remain childless.

But the god of war, Mars, fell in love with Rhea Silvia and made her the mother of twins: Romulus and Remus. Fearing the future heirs, Amulius ordered the babies to be killed. The soldiers, moved by pity, left them floating in a basket on the Tiber.

A she-wolf, hearing their cries, found the twins and fed them in her den on Palatine Hill. Later, a shepherd and his wife adopted them. When they grew up, Romulus and Remus killed Amulius and gave back the throne to their grandfather Numitor. Then they founded a new city on the very spot where the she-wolf had saved them.

Some believe the "she-wolf" was actually their adoptive mother, as the word lupa also meant "prostitute" in ancient slang.

The legend also tells how Romulus killed Remus. The two brothers disagreed on which hill should be the center of their new city. Following the Etruscan ritual of bird watching (augury), Remus saw six birds, but Romulus saw twelve above Palatine Hill. Romulus drew the boundary of the city with a plough and declared that no one should cross it. Remus mocked him and crossed the line. Romulus then killed him, becoming the first king of Rome, in 754 BC, according to ancient sources.

Today, the ancient city of Alba Longa is identified with Albano Laziale, a town in the Castelli Romani area, southeast of Rome.
