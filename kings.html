The Kings of Rome

Numa Pompilius (716–673 BC): Of Sabine origin, a peaceful king. He founded many religious rites, priesthoods, and reformed the Roman calendar.

Tullus Hostilius (673–641 BC): A warlike king, known for conquering Alba Longa.

Ancus Marcius (640–616 BC): Founder of the port city of Ostia. He built Rome’s first bridge, the Pons Sublicius.

Tarquinius Priscus (616–578 BC): Of Etruscan origin, he commissioned monumental, civil, and sacred works. Achieved important military victories against neighboring peoples.

Servius Tullius (578–534 BC): Possibly of Etruscan origin. He carried out a major constitutional reform by creating the Centuriate Assembly and reorganizing Rome’s territorial tribes.

Tarquinius Superbus (534–509 BC): Involved in the murder of Servius Tullius, he ruled Rome as a tyrant. He was eventually overthrown by a group of aristocrats who established a republican form of government.
